Privacy Policy

Short version: your API key is stored only as a hash, never in plaintext. In order to tell you which field changed and what it changed from, we keep a plaintext copy of feed entries on our server's disk — that is a requirement of the capability you are paying for. We do not sell, share, profile, or link your data to anyone else's.

Last updated: 17 October 2026

What we actually keep: a SHA-256 hash of your API key, the monitor URLs and names you gave us, the titles and bodies of the feed entries you monitor (plaintext), error messages, and the domain part of the email you used to obtain a key. Except for your API key's plaintext, all of this lives on our server's disk in a file readable only by the service process.

The complete, machine-readable inventory: GET /v1/data-handling (public, no authentication required). That endpoint is checked against the actual on-disk fields by an automated test; if this page and that endpoint ever disagree, the endpoint is authoritative and this page will be corrected.

Why do we store feed entries in plaintext? Because the core capability we promise is telling you which field changed and what it changed from, rather than merely “there is an update”. To output “the title went from A to B”, both A and B must exist on disk. If we stored only hashes, they would be irreversible and we could tell you that something changed but never what it changed to — which degrades the product into a generic “this page changed” notifier, the very thing we are not selling.

This content is itself a public feed, at an address you gave us. We cache it only to compute your diff.

What we additionally record (counts only, never your data)

To keep the product running we need to know how many people use it and how many get stuck. So we record some purely aggregate facts:

Hitting the 5-monitor limitSame + count at the timeTo know how many people need more quota
Monitor creation failureFailure category (not content, e.g. "not_feed")To find product gaps

These statistics records contain none of:

· your monitor URLs
· feed titles, bodies or authors
· the changes we detect
· your email address
· your API key (plaintext or hash)
· request headers, browser fingerprint or source IP

In other words: we know that 3 people hit the limit, but not who they are or what sites they monitor.

Please note the distinction: the list above describes the operational statistics log (events-*.jsonl), which really does contain only aggregate counts. Your monitors and feed content are stored on disk — see the “What we store, and for how long” table above. These two facts do not conflict: the statistics log does not record your data, but the service itself caches your data in order to compute your diffs.

Why this deserves its own section

Most monitoring tools embed third-party trackers. We do not — so this list is the complete inventory; there is no second, hidden analytics system.

Three things worth spelling out

Your API key is never stored in plaintext

When you create a key, the response contains it exactly once. From then on we keep only SHA-256(key) and compare against that. Even someone with full read access to our server data files cannot reconstruct your key’s plaintext from what we hold.

The trade-off is honest: we cannot show you a lost key, and we cannot email it back to you. If you lose it, you create a new one.

What we cache, why, and what we never do with it

To provide field-level changes — telling you the title went from A to B, rather than just “there is an update” — we must keep the previous copy of the feed on disk to compare against. This is a requirement of the capability, not a choice we made.

What we never do:

  • We do not analyse, resell, or share this content with anyone
  • We do not profile you, and we do not link data across customers
  • Push notifications contain only hashes and lengths — never full entry text
  • We do not use advertising networks, analytics trackers, or third-party data brokers

This content is itself a public feed, at an address you gave us. We cache it only to compute your diff.

How long we keep it, and how to delete it

Your data is kept for as long as your monitor exists, with the baseline overwritten on every check. When you delete a monitor, its cached feed content is deleted with it.

You can do these yourself, at any time:

  • Check that the service is actually working — call GET /v1/monitors. Every monitor returns last_checked_at, last_check_ok, last_error and next_check_at. If last_check_ok is true and last_error is null, the most recent check succeeded.
  • Delete a monitor and its cached feed content — DELETE /v1/monitors/{id}

⚠️ Two things we cannot do self-service, and we would rather say so than let you assume a button exists:

  • Revoking a key — there is no self-service endpoint yet; email us.
  • Deleting your entire account data (including your API key hash and email domain) — currently performed manually by us; email us and we will do it within 1 business day.

Payments

Payments are handled entirely by our payment provider. We receive confirmation that a charge succeeded and the plan that was bought — never your full card or bank details.

Security measures

  • Webhooks carry an HMAC-SHA256 signature so you can verify we sent the payload, and can reject anything forged.
  • The Node service binds to 127.0.0.1 only; TLS terminates at the reverse proxy.
  • All outbound fetches go through an SSRF guard with DNS pinning and per-hop revalidation, so the service cannot be used to reach private networks or cloud metadata endpoints.
  • Feed bodies are capped at 10 MB, and XML entity expansion is structurally bounded.

Your rights

You can, at any time:

  • Self-service: delete any monitor you created (DELETE /v1/monitors/{id}) — its cached feed content is deleted with it
  • Self-service: check the service's status (GET /v1/monitors) — each monitor's last check time, success flag and error message
  • Ask what data we hold about you — GET /v1/data-handling lists every field name; email us for the actual values
  • Request deletion of your account data — this one currently requires manual handling, see the section above

⚠️ We state the limits of what currently works honestly: there is no self-service “delete everything” endpoint, and no self-service key revocation. Both require an email to us. We are telling you here rather than letting you assume a button exists.
